# Week 13 — Exploration/Reflection: AI Artists

## Artist and project: Memo Akten, *Learning to See* (2017)

I chose Memo Akten’s *Learning to See* because it makes the process of machine learning visible instead of presenting AI as something mysterious that simply understands images. The interactive work points a camera at everyday objects and uses neural networks to reconstruct the scene based on patterns learned from training images. What interested me most is that the system does not reproduce objects exactly as they are; it interprets them through the examples and visual patterns contained in its training data. Akten describes different custom models trained on datasets representing ocean waves, clouds and sky, fire, flowers, and images from the Hubble Space Telescope. The project makes me question how much of an AI output comes from the live input and how much comes from the choices made when building the training dataset. The artist identifies the sources and themes of several datasets, but the project descriptions do not fully explain who performed all of the collection, tagging, and labeling work. This absence matters because the labor and decisions behind a model can shape what it recognizes and what it ignores. I also like that the audience can move objects in front of the camera and immediately see how the model transforms them, making the system’s limitations part of the experience. The project connects to everyday AI tools because it reminds me that a model’s output should not be confused with an objective understanding of the world.
