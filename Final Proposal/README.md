## 2. Concept
Clock in Space explores how time can be experienced as a spatial and visual event rather than only read as numbers on a screen. I want to develop a clock animation that responds to the geometry of a physical surface. Instead of placing a standard clock face onto a wall or object, the movement of time will become a sequence of lines, pulses, and shifting forms that wrap around mapped surfaces.

## 3. How the new work will respond to the earlier work
The earlier clock sketch provides the underlying time-based logic, while the projection-mapping exercise provides the physical context. The new version will develop a more deliberate visual system and use the shape of the projection surface as part of the composition. Projection should become part of the concept rather than simply a larger display.

## 8. Expected challenges
The main challenge will be making the animation legible on an irregular or multi-part surface. I will need to test how projection changes color, contrast, and scale, and how the animation can remain coherent when it crosses between surfaces. I also expect to spend time refining the mapping and adjusting the composition to the actual room and projector.
